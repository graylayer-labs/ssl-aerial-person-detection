# #68 Thermal input ablation

Set out to: test whether thermal frames, single-channel heat maps, need preparing differently for a backbone trained on photographs. Three arms on the thermal view at 10% and 100% of labels, all four folds: copy the channel (default), histogram-equalise first, or learn a small input stem.
Chose: equalisation as its own feature cache, part of the cache's identity so arms cannot be mixed. The stem sits before the frozen backbone, so no cache can serve it; cold, it costs about 6 h per run on the laptop. So it starts from the trained #66 head and is compared with a control trained the same 120 extra steps without the stem.
Expected before running: equalisation to help a little (it is the usual "make thermal look like a photo" step), the stem to help a little more.
Surprising:
- Equalisation hurt on three of four folds at both fractions (mean `ap_iou25` −0.050 at 10%, −0.137 at 100%). A person in thermal is a warm spot; equalising lifts the background and flattens it.
- The stem did nothing measurable (−0.009, +0.007).
- The control itself was a finding: 120 more steps lifted the 10% heads from 0.129 to 0.162. Comparing the stem with the cold head would have credited it with that.
- Review caught two bugs that would have biased the stem arm before any run: micro-batches weighted by frames while the loss normalises by people, and the stem drawing random numbers before the head was built, so its head started from different weights at the same seed.
- A re-run of one #66 cell from today's main reproduced it bit-for-bit, weights included.
